### Progressive Overload Strategy

**How to progress (in order of priority):**

1. **Increase reps** within your target range (e.g., 4 reps → 5 → 6 at same weight)
2. **Increase weight** when you hit the top of rep range with 2+ RIR (add 2.5-5 lbs upper body, 5-10 lbs lower body)
3. **Increase sets** by 1 per muscle group every 2-4 weeks (cap at ~18 sets/week per muscle)
4. **Improve range of motion** — a deeper squat at the same weight is more stimulus
5. **Slow the eccentric** — 3-4 second lowering phase increases tension without adding weight

**Deload every 4-6 weeks:**
- Reduce volume (sets) by 40-50%
- Reduce load by 10-15%
- Maintain exercise selection and frequency
- Keep stability and mobility work at full volume

## 4. Nutrition Plan

### Caloric Strategy: Moderate Deficit for Body Recomposition

At 24% body fat with lower lean mass, you are in an ideal position for body recomposition (simultaneous fat loss + muscle gain).

**Step 1: Estimate Maintenance Calories**
- Body weight (lbs) x 14-16 = estimated maintenance
- Example: 185 lbs x 15 = ~2,775 kcal/day
- Adjust based on actual weight trends after 2 weeks

**Step 2: Apply Moderate Deficit**
- Target: **300-500 kcal below maintenance**
- Example: 2,775 - 400 = ~2,375 kcal/day
- Never go below BMR or create a deficit > 750 kcal

**Why moderate:** Aggressive deficits cause muscle loss, hormonal disruption, and metabolic slowdown — the opposite of recomposition.

### Macronutrient Targets

| Macro | Target | For 84 kg (185 lb) male at ~2,375 kcal |
|-------|--------|----------------------------------------|
| **Protein** | 1.8-2.2 g/kg/day | **150-185 g/day** (600-740 kcal) |
| **Fat** | 0.8-1.0 g/kg/day | **67-84 g/day** (600-756 kcal) |
| **Carbs** | Remaining calories | **~220-290 g/day** (880-1160 kcal) |

### Protein Priority

Protein is the #1 nutritional priority for body recomposition at 42:
- Age-related "anabolic resistance" means muscles need MORE protein stimulus, not less
- During a caloric deficit, protein requirements increase further
- Research threshold: >1.3 g/kg prevents muscle decline; 1.8-2.2 g/kg optimizes growth

**Distribution:** Spread protein across 4-5 meals, each providing 30-40g:
- Meal 1 (morning): 35-40g
- Meal 2 (lunch): 35-40g
- Meal 3 (pre-training): 35-40g
- Meal 4 (post-training): 35-40g
- Meal 5 (optional, pre-sleep): 20-30g (casein-based: cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, casein shake)

### Key Micronutrients for 40+ Males

| Nutrient | Why | Sources / Supplementation |
|----------|-----|---------------------------|
| Vitamin D | Testosterone support, bone density, immune function | Test levels; supplement 2,000-5,000 IU/day if deficient |
| Zinc | Testosterone synthesis, immune function | Red meat, oysters, pumpkin seeds; or 15-30 mg/day |
| Magnesium | Muscle function, sleep quality, recovery | Leafy greens, nuts; or 200-400 mg glycinate before bed |
| Omega-3 | Anti-inflammatory, joint health, cardiovascular | Fatty fish 2-3x/week or 2-3g EPA/DHA daily |
| Creatine | Strength, muscle mass, cognitive function | 5g creatine monohydrate daily (most researched supplement) |

### Hydration
- Target: 3-4 liters per day
- Additional 500ml per hour of training
- Monitor urine color (pale yellow = adequate)

## 7. Adjustment Protocols

### Daily: Autoregulation via RPE

If your program calls for sets at RPE 8 (2 reps in reserve):
- Today's weight feels like RPE 9 → reduce load by ~5%
- Today's weight feels like RPE 7 → increase load by ~5%

### Traffic Light System (Using Wearable Data)

| Status | HRV vs 30-day avg | Action |
|--------|-------------------|--------|
| **Green** | > 95% of baseline | Train as planned — full intensity and volume |
| **Yellow** | 90-95% of baseline | Reduce volume by 1-2 sets/exercise OR intensity by 5-10% |
| **Red** | < 90% of baseline | Active recovery day OR dramatically reduced session |

### When to Deload (reactive triggers — don't wait for schedule if these appear)

Deload immediately if 2+ of these occur simultaneously:
- RPE rising on same loads for 2+ weeks
- HRV consistently below baseline for 5+ days
- Sleep quality declining
- Subjective wellness consistently below 5/10
- Resting HR elevated 5+ bpm above baseline

### Plateau-Breaking (if a lift stalls for 3+ weeks)

1. **First:** Check recovery — sleep, nutrition, stress, HRV
2. **Then:** Try changing rep range (if stuck at 5 reps, try 3x8 for 3 weeks)
3. **Then:** Try exercise variation (front squat instead of back squat for a cycle)
4. **Then:** Add 1-2 sets per week to that muscle group
5. **Last resort:** Take a full deload week, then restart 10% below plateau weight

### Monthly Program Adjustments

| Signal | Meaning | Adjustment |
|--------|---------|------------|
| Strength increasing + body comp improving | Working | Continue; consider small volume increase |
| Strength stalling + body comp unchanged | Plateau | Vary rep ranges, add volume, change exercise selection |
| Strength declining + fatigue increasing | Overreaching | Immediate deload; reduce volume 2-4 weeks |
| Strength increasing + body fat increasing | Surplus too large | Tighten calorie deficit by 100-200 kcal |
| Good recovery + no strength gains | Under-training | Add 1-2 sets/muscle/week; push RPE to 8-9 |

### Red Flags — Stop and Reassess

| Warning Sign | Action |
|-------------|--------|
| Joint pain persisting > 48 hours post-training | Modify exercise; see physiotherapist if 1+ week |
| Sharp or shooting pain during any movement | STOP that exercise immediately; get checked |
| Resting HR elevated 5+ bpm for multiple days | Extra rest; reduce training load |
| Persistent irritability, brain fog, no motivation | Major overtraining signal — deload and assess all stressors |
| Frequent illness or slow healing | Immune suppression from overtraining — reduce load, prioritize nutrition/sleep |

### Concurrent Training: Minimizing Interference

Research confirms you can build strength AND improve VO2 max, but smart programming matters:
- Separate HIIT and heavy leg days by 24+ hours (already done in the schedule above)
- If you must combine: do strength FIRST, then cardio
- HIIT causes LESS interference than long steady-state cardio
- Keeping cardio sessions under 30 min minimizes interference (our HIIT is ~43 min but most is recovery)

### Hormonal Considerations at 42

- Testosterone declines 1-3% per year after 40
- Resistance training (especially squats and deadlifts) still produces significant acute testosterone increases
- Exercise alone generates superior outcomes to testosterone replacement therapy for strength, fitness, and body composition in men with low-normal testosterone
- Sleep 7-9 hours (5 hours of sleep reduces testosterone by 10-15%)
- Manage stress (chronic cortisol suppresses testosterone)
- Getting from 24% to 18% body fat itself improves hormonal profile (less aromatase activity)
- Consider blood work to establish hormonal baseline

### The Polarized Training Model

Research shows the optimal distribution of training intensity:
- **80% of cardio volume in Zone 2** (low intensity)
- **20% of cardio volume in Zone 4-5** (high intensity)
- **Minimal time in Zone 3** ("gray zone" — too hard for base, too easy for VO2 max)

With 2 cardio sessions/week, the Wednesday HIIT (~16 min of Zone 5 work) and Saturday Zone 2 (60-90 min) approximate this ratio.
